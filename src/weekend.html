Fayette, Maine

The
Weekend

Most weddings give you a Saturday.
This is a weekend.

Friday Evening

Arrive. Settle in.

Cars pull in through the afternoon. Someone finds the kitchen. Welcome drinks happen in the courtyard without an agenda — no program, no wristbands, no one telling you where to stand.

The weekend starts before it officially starts.

Saturday

The main event.

Pick your spot — open field with panoramic views, or a wooded clearing nestled into the trees. Wooden arch built by hand. Rows of benches. The land does most of the heavy lifting.

Everyone you want there.
Actually there.

Cocktail Hour

The bar's open. Golden hour's here.

The ceremony ends and everyone exhales. Live music fills the field. Guests drift between the fire pits, the lawn, and the courtyard. Golden hour takes care of the rest.

Dinner

Food trucks, firelight.

Dinner can be whatever you're picturing — food trucks in the courtyard, a caterer in the barn, a full plated service. The space works for all of it.

Late Night

The part nobody forgets.

The barn lights low. The food window still glowing. Someone swapped their shoes hours ago. The party finds its own pace.

Bedtime

Walk back to the farmhouse.

When you're ready, the farmhouse is a short walk away. You fall asleep under the same roof as your closest people, and nobody has to drive anywhere.

Sunday Morning

Slow down. Linger a little.

Nobody's in a hurry. The farmhouse kitchen fills up slowly — coffee, last night's stories, someone piecing together the timeline.

Most weddings end in a parking lot at midnight. This one ends on a Sunday morning. Checkout is at midday.
